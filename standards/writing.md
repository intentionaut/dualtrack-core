# Interface words

Every word a person reads in the product: labels, buttons, messages, help, notices. Numbered WR-n.

1. **Front-load**: the point first, in the first few words.
2. **Their words**: the audience's terms, never internal names, codes or jargon.
3. **One term per thing**: the same name for the same thing everywhere.
4. **Actions as verbs**: buttons and commands say what happens: "Save draft", not "OK".
5. **Errors**: what happened, then what to do. No blame, no "oops", no raw codes alone.
6. **Short**: one idea per sentence; cut words that do not change the meaning.
7. **Sentence case**, and plain punctuation.
8. **Numbers and dates** in the form the audience uses.
9. **No dead ends**: every message that stops a person says the next step.
10. **Experience first**: say what the person gets and what changes for them, not how it works. No mechanics, internals or metaphors a non-technical reader must decode. How it works belongs in docs written for engineers.
